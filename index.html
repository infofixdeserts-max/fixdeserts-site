Stage 01 — the starting point

The arid, broken land

Bare desert. Crusted soil. No water cycle. This is where so much of America's drylands sit today — and where the cattle already live.

The Beautiful Side Effect

Feed people well. Grow soil back.

The very method that makes food healthy also regenerates the land it's raised on. It's not a trade-off — it's one system doing both.

Ferment biodiverse forage

Grasses and plants are cut, compressed, and fermented — boosting nutrition and neutralizing toxins before they're ever fed.

Feed it to the herd

Healthy cattle thrive — growing faster with less feed — and their dung & urine become the life-giving "biocarpet" on the soil.

The biocarpet works

A thick layer of dung & urine blocks the sun's sterilizing UV rays, holds moisture, and reseeds life — microbes, fungi, then grass roots.

Green = water

Regenerated land cools itself and restarts the micro water cycle — raising rainfall, refilling aquifers, cooling whole valleys.

Desert becomes grassland

Exactly the side effect we're after: healthy food for people, and living, breathing land restored in the process.

Once we green at least 150,000 acres around the Salton Sea, we change the micro water cycle — producing 8–9 inches of rain a year, cooling the region, and replenishing the aquifers.

Their system takes a beef animal from kill floor to truck in 24 hours, bones a full carcass into primals in under 20 minutes with one worker, uses no splitting saws and no spinal-cord exposure — cutting labor 30–50%, cooling meat twice as fast as industry standard, and lowering energy cost and shrinkage. New boneless (game-style) meat processing like this is what lets small regional plants in ten states handle 5.6 million animals a year.

We grow biodiverse, soil-building forage nearby under irrigation, cut it, and compress it into silage or feed it fresh — trucked to the herd within 2–3 hours of cutting. It's the fermented forage that makes the meat healthy and begins regenerating the soil.
